## TRENETTE AL PESTO

Trenette al Pesto is a traditional pasta dish from Genoa in the Liguria region of Italy, famously featured in the Disney-Pixar movie Luca.

Traditional Ingredients

• Pasta: 500g trenette or linguine
• Vegetables: 1 large peeled and diced potato, plus 100g green beans cut into short pieces
• Pesto Genovese: Fresh basil leaves, garlic cloves, pine nuts, coarse sea salt, extra-virgin olive oil, Parmigiano Reggiano, and Pecorino Romano.

How to Make It

1. Make the Pesto: Crush or blend garlic, pine nuts, and coarse salt, then add fresh basil and slowly stream in extra-virgin olive oil and grated cheeses until creamy.
2. Boil the Veggies and Pasta: Cook the diced potatoes and green beans in a large pot of salted boiling water along with the trenette pasta so everything finishes together.
3. Combine: Reserve a splash of starchy pasta water, drain the pasta and vegetables, and toss them off the heat with the pesto and pasta water to create a silky, glossy coating.

## LASAGNA

Lasagna al forno is a classic Italian baked pasta dish composed of layers of thin egg pasta sheets, a slow-simmered meat ragù, creamy besciamella (béchamel) sauce, and grated Parmigiano-Reggiano cheese. Traditional Northern Italian versions (specifically from Emilia-Romagna) rely heavily on the balance of these components rather than heavy layers of cheese, entirely skipping the ricotta commonly found in Italian-American variations.

🛒 Key Ingredients

• Pasta: Fresh egg lasagna sheets.
• The Ragù: A combination of ground beef and ground pork cooked with a aromatic soffritto (onion, carrot, celery), pancetta, white or red wine, tomato passata, and beef stock.
• The Besciamella: A velvety white sauce made from a butter and flour roux, whole milk, and a pinch of ground nutmeg.
• The Topping: Freshly grated Parmigiano-Reggiano or Grana Padano cheese.


👩‍🍳 Traditional Recipe & Steps


1. Simmer the Ragù

• Sauté the aromatics: Heat olive oil in a Dutch oven and gently cook finely chopped onion, carrot, and celery until soft. Add diced pancetta and cook until the fat renders.
• Brown the meat: Add the ground beef and pork, breaking it apart, and cook until no longer pink.
• Deglaze: Pour in dry red or white wine and let it reduce by half.
• Simmer: Stir in tomato paste, passata, a cup of stock, and bay leaves. Cover and simmer on low for 2 to 4 hours, adding more stock as needed to keep it silky and loose.

2. Whisk the Besciamella

• Make the roux: Melt butter in a saucepan over medium heat, then whisk in the flour and cook for 1–2 minutes until pale blond.
• Thicken: Slowly stream in warm whole milk while whisking continuously to prevent lumps.
• Season: Simmer until velvety and thick enough to coat the back of a spoon, then stir in salt, black pepper, and a generous pinch of freshly grated nutmeg.

3. Assemble the Layers

• Base: Spread a thin layer of ragù on the bottom of a 9x13 inch baking dish.
• Build: Place a layer of pasta sheets, followed by a thin smear of ragù, a drizzle of besciamella, and a handful of grated Parmigiano.
• Repeat: Build 5 to 8 thin layers in this exact order.
• Top: Finish with a final layer of pasta completely covered in besciamella, a touch of ragù, and plenty of Parmigiano.

4. Bake to Golden Perfection

• Preheat your oven to 350°F (180°C).
• Cover the dish with foil and bake for 30 to 40 minutes.
• Remove the foil and bake for another 10 to 15 minutes until the top is bubbling and deeply golden brown.
• Crucial Step: Let the lasagna rest for 15 to 20 minutes before slicing so the layers set properly and don't slide apart.


## TIRAMISU

Tiramisu is a world-famous, no-bake Italian dessert made of coffee-dipped ladyfingers (savoiardi) layered with a rich whipped cream of mascarpone cheese, egg yolks, and sugar, all dusted with cocoa powder. Originating in the Veneto region of Italy, its name literally translates to "pick me up," a nod to the energy boost from the strong espresso and sugar.

🛒 Key Ingredients

• Savoiardi: Authentic Italian ladyfingers. They are crisp, airy, and structured to absorb the coffee without turning into mush.
• Mascarpone: A thick, velvety, and mild Italian cream cheese that forms the base of the filling.
• Eggs: Traditional recipes use raw egg yolks whipped with sugar (and often folded whipped whites or heavy cream for extra fluffiness).
• Espresso: High-quality, strong brewed espresso, cooled to room temperature.
• Liquor (Optional): Marsala wine is the traditional choice, though dark rum or coffee liqueurs like Kahlúa are widely popular.
• Cocoa Powder: Unsweetened Dutch-process cocoa powder for a bitter contrast on top.

👩‍🍳 Traditional Recipe & Steps


1. Prepare the Coffee Bath

• Brew about 1.5 cups of strong espresso.
• Pour it into a wide, shallow bowl so it's easy to dip the ladyfingers.
• If desired, stir in 2 tablespoons of Marsala wine or rum and let the mixture cool completely.

2. Whip the Mascarpone Cream

• The Sabayon Base: In a large bowl, whisk egg yolks and granulated sugar together until pale yellow and thick (about 3–5 minutes). For safety, you can do this over a double boiler (bain-marie) until the sugar dissolves.
• Add Mascarpone: Gently fold room-temperature mascarpone cheese into the egg mixture until completely smooth and free of lumps.
• Lighten it up: In a separate bowl, whip heavy cream (or egg whites) to stiff peaks, then gently fold it into the mascarpone mixture to create a light, cloud-like cream.

3. Dip and Layer

• The Dip: Quickly submerge each ladyfinger into the coffee for just 1 to 2 seconds per side. Do not let them soak, or the dessert will become watery.
• First Layer: Arrange the dipped ladyfingers tightly in a single layer at the bottom of an 8x8 or 9x13-inch dish.
• The Cream: Spread half of the mascarpone cream smoothly over the ladyfinger layer.
• Repeat: Add a second layer of dipped ladyfingers running perpendicular to the first layer, then top with the remaining mascarpone cream.

4. Chill and Serve

• Cover the dish and refrigerate for at least 4 to 6 hours (overnight is best) to let the flavors meld and the structure set up.
• Just before serving, use a fine mesh sieve to dust a generous, even layer of cocoa powder over the top.
